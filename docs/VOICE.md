# How Motion Audio writes

The website, the help guides, the manuals and the docs should read like Sam wrote them. Sam is the person who makes Central, explaining it to another church's worship leader or tech person. They shouldn't read like a product page a machine filled in.

## Whose voice

- **I** is Sam, the person who makes Central. Use it where a person would naturally speak for themselves: the home page intro, a note at the top of a guide, a recommendation ("If you're new to this, I'd start with a church pack"), an honest limit ("I haven't got the iPhone app into the App Store yet").
- **You** is the reader. Most sentences are about what you do and what you'll see. Don't put "I" in every line.
- **We** is fine for things the company does, like taking payments, emailing keys, or "we grade every upload".
- Never make things up about Sam: no church name, no backstory, no years, no "my worship team", no customer quotes, no numbers that aren't real. If a story would help, leave a spot for Sam to add one rather than inventing it.

## How it should sound

- Write like you talk. Use contractions. Use short words. Mix short sentences with longer ones.
- Be specific. Say "the footswitch on the far left", not "intuitive controls". Say what happens: "the pads fade out over four seconds".
- Say it once. Don't end a section by summing it up again.
- Put the useful part first. Lead with what most people need, then the details.
- Be honest about rough edges. If something needs a workaround, say so plainly and say what to do.
- Keep facts exact. Prices, plan names, menu paths, button labels and app names must match the apps and the price list. Write menu paths the way they are now: Settings → Sound → Test sound.

## What to avoid (these are what make text read like AI)

- **Lists of three, everywhere.** "Lights, lyrics and in-ears" is fine once. Ten of them in a row reads like a machine. Vary it: two things, four things, or one thing said well.
- **Label-colon bullets.** "Lights: run every fixture…", "Slides: …". Write a sentence instead, or use a plain list.
- **Bold lead-ins on every bullet**, and bullets for things that read better as a short paragraph.
- **Perfectly parallel structure:** every card the same length, every line starting with a verb, every heading the same shape.
- **Stock phrases:** "seamless", "effortless", "unlock", "elevate", "powerful", "robust", "and more", "everything you need, nothing you don't", "whether you're X or Y", "from X to Y", "no more X", "say goodbye to", "that's it", "simply", "designed to", "built for", "take it to the next level".
- **Headline formulas:** a question followed by an answer ("Made a great track? Share it and get paid."), and catchy two-part slogans.
- **Em dashes and semicolons** used as decoration. Use a full stop.
- **Parenthetical asides in every sentence.** One now and then is fine.
- **"Every" and "each" in every sentence.** "Every app, every device, every song" is a tell.
- **Version tags in what customers read:** "(10.4)", "From 10.2 …". Release notes are the place for versions.
- **Hedging and filler:** "This allows you to", "In order to", "It's worth noting that", "Please note".

## Manuals and help guides

- Open with one or two sentences: what this guide gets you, what you need on hand, and roughly how long it takes.
- Number the steps when the order matters. One action per step, followed by what you'll see.
- Say what to do when it goes wrong, at the step where it goes wrong ("If the screen stays white, …").
- Use the button and menu names exactly as they appear in the app.
- End when the job is done. No recap.

## Before and after

> **Before:** Central Production: a DAW and sampler for making tracks, loops, samples, pads, instruments, plugins and pedal boards. Free; your sound plan unlocks the better sounds.
>
> **After:** Central Production is where I make the pads and tracks that ship with Central, and you can use it too. Record a song, build a pad from a piano note, or wire up your own reverb. It's free. If you have a sound plan, the better sounds show up inside it.

> **Before:** Plugin response: Fast (about 100 ms), Balanced (about 200 ms) or Safe (about 400 ms).
>
> **After:** Plugin response sets how quickly you hear a knob change while the song plays. Balanced is right for most computers. If you've got a fast one, try Fast. If the sound breaks up, go to Safe.
