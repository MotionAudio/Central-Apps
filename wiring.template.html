Pad Pedal Wiring

Everything you solder to the Pi: 7 pad footswitches, Stop, Tap tempo and Next song switches, 7 pad LEDs, a rotary encoder, 4 knobs and an expression-pedal jack. These pin numbers are exactly what the Pad Pedal driver expects out of the box.

3.3 V onlyEverything here runs from 3.3 V (pin 1 or 17). Never connect 5 V (pins 2, 4) to a GPIO, the encoder or the ADS1115 boards.

System overview

Screen: the official 7″ touchscreen (DSI ribbon) or any HDMI touchscreen. Audio: the 3.5 mm jack works, but a USB audio interface or an I2S DAC HAT sounds much cleaner. GPIO 18–21 are left free so a DAC HAT fits without moving anything.

Footswitches and pad LEDs

That is one pad channel. Wire all seven the same way using the table, then wire Stop, Tap tempo and Next song like a footswitch with no LED. Tap and Next use the serial-port pins (GPIO 14 and 15); they are free as long as you never turn on the serial console. One shared ground wire can run along the whole row of switches and LEDs.

What the switches do: a pad switch plays that pad (press it again to fade out). Hold one pad switch and press another to layer the two. Stop fades out; hold it 1 s to cut instantly. Tap sets the tempo; hold it 1 s to freeze the sound. Next song steps forward; hold it 1 s to go back. LEDs: every playing pad's LED stays lit; when nothing plays, the selected pad blinks every 1.5 s.

Knobs (ADS1115 #1)

Outer lugs go to GND and 3.3 V; the middle lug goes to the ADS input. If a knob works backwards, swap its two outer lugs (or add its name to "invert" in hardware.json). Take the pots' 3.3 V and GND from the ADS board's VDD/GND or from Pi pin 17 and any GND pin.

Expression pedal (ADS1115 #2)

Both ADS1115 boards share the same four wires (3.3 V, GND, SCL, SDA); only the ADDR pin differs. The 1 kΩ resistor protects the Pi from the brief short when a plug goes in. Use a TRS expression pedal with the tip as the wiper (Roland EV-5, Boss FV-500L, M-Audio EX-P on its "M-Audio" setting). The driver calibrates itself: rock the pedal heel-to-toe once after power-on. If it works upside down, add "expression" to "invert" in hardware.json.

Rotary encoder

A bare encoder (no little board) works too: connect its two outer pins to {{ENC_A}} and {{ENC_B}}, its middle pin to GND, and its push-switch between {{ENC_SW}} and GND. If turning goes the wrong way, swap the CLK and DT wires.

Parts list

Core

  • Raspberry Pi 4 (2 GB or more) + official 5 V 3 A USB-C supply
  • microSD card, 16 GB or larger (Class 10 / A1)
  • Official Raspberry Pi 7″ touchscreen, or any HDMI touchscreen
  • Audio: USB audio interface or I2S DAC HAT (recommended), or the 3.5 mm jack. A USB audio interface with an input also lets you run guitar or keys through the effects
  • Optional: USB MIDI keyboard or MIDI foot controller; USB stick or USB SD-card reader for sounds

Control surface

  • 10 × momentary soft-touch footswitches (SPST, normally open): 7 pads, Stop, Tap, Next song
  • 7 × LEDs (3 or 5 mm) + 7 × 330 Ω resistors (100 Ω for blue/white)
  • 1 × KY-040 rotary encoder module with knob
  • 4 × 10 kΩ linear pots (B10K) + knobs
  • 2 × ADS1115 16-bit ADC breakout boards
  • 1 × ¼″ stereo (TRS) panel jack, 1 × 1 kΩ resistor, 1 × 100 nF capacitor
  • Hook-up wire (22–26 AWG), female Dupont jumpers or a 40-pin ribbon + breakout
  • An enclosure or pedal-board chassis

Power-on checks

Do these over SSH (ssh pi@padpedal.local) after the first boot with the hardware connected.

i2cdetect -y 1

You should see 48 and 49 in the grid. Missing one? Check that board's SDA/SCL wires and its ADDR pin.

journalctl -u pad-pedal-driver -f

Live log. Every footswitch press, encoder turn and knob move prints a line. Press Ctrl+C to stop watching.

sudo nano /etc/pad-pedal/hardware.json && sudo systemctl restart pad-pedal-driver

Wired something to a different pin? Change the number here instead of re-soldering. Numbers are GPIO numbers, not pin numbers.
