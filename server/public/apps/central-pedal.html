What it is

The Central Pedal sits on the floor in front of whoever leads the band. It plays your pads and multitracks, with the click and spoken cues, shows the chart and the lyrics, has a looper, and can be the rig you plug your guitar, bass or keyboard into. You run it with your feet, the knobs and sliders, and a 7-inch touch screen.

Inside is a Raspberry Pi Compute Module 5 on a board we designed for it. We put each one together, load the software, test every control and send it out ready to play. Switch it on and it goes straight into the pedal app.

On top there are 8 footswitches along the front, 14 knobs that turn all the way round and press, 4 sliders, the 7-inch touch screen and a 5-inch screen beside it. Every footswitch, knob and slider has a little screen next to it that says what it does. On the back there's the power button and USB-C, the network jack (NET), six outputs (OUT 1-2, 3-4 and 5-6), two instrument inputs (INST A and B) and four USB ports, each pair with its own label screen. On the right side there's the I/O port for Central I/O.

If you've got a computer running Central Pedal Control, it keeps your songs and sends the pedal one set at a time. If you don't, the pedal plays everything itself.

Sounds you add

The pedal doesn't need a key. It's yours, it gets every update, and the starter sounds are free.

The sound plans add pianos, synths, instruments, effects, guitar pedals and pads. Each plan comes as a package key in your email, and it's under My products on your account too. On the pedal, go to Settings → Sound plans, paste the key in and press Add. Then install the libraries you want under Settings → Sound libraries. Every pad, loop and preset has a 30-second preview, so you can hear it before you download it.

One package key works on three of your devices at once, as long as each is a different app, for example your pedal, your Central Control computer and your Central Usage tablet. Every plan includes Central Pads, which are pads in every key made for this pedal.

First time on

Your pedal arrives with the software already on it. It takes a few minutes to set up, all on the 7-inch screen.

  1. Plug the USB-C power supply into the back and plug in your outputs. Press the power button next to it.
  2. The internet. Pick your church's Wi-Fi and type its password on the screen's keyboard. If you'd rather use a cable, plug it into NET on the back.
  3. A name for this pedal, the way the rest of your team's apps will see it, like "Grace Church - Main room".
  4. The church account. Sign in so the pedal can get your setlists, Central Tracks, CCLI and Planning Center. They all use the same sign-in.

You can skip any step and do it later in Settings. To pair the pedal with Central Control or Central Pedal Control, set its 4-digit PIN under Settings → This pedal's PIN. If your church uses Central Control, use the main admin's code.

Moving to a new building? Settings → Wi-Fi joins another network, or press Run the first start again under Settings → Central Pedal.

The two screens

The 7-inch is the touch screen. Its tabs run along the top: LIVE, Pads, Tracks, Instrument, Looper, Configure, Learn, I/O and Settings. Everything else (the mixer, scenes, the song editor, practice mixes, the stem splitter, the service report) is under ⋯. The 🔍 button finds a song, a setlist or any setting and takes you straight there.

The 5-inch is for reading from a step back, so its pages are bigger and have nothing to press. It can show the stage view (the section you're in, the beat, what's next and the time left), the lyrics, the chord chart, a part, the setlist, the meters, or any of the playing pages. It shows one page or two side by side. To send what's on the 7-inch over to it, press → 5″ at the top, or pick what it shows under Settings → Screens.

Settings → Brightness has a slider for each screen and one for all the label screens together.

The controls and their labels

Each control's label screen shows its job right now, and for a knob or slider how far it's turned. Give a control a new job and its label changes with it. Here's what they do out of the box:

ControlWhat it does
Footswitches 1 to 4PREV section (hold: previous song), PLAY (hold: start over), STOP (hold: stop everything at once), NEXT section (hold: next song)
Footswitches 5 to 8PAD plays the pad you've picked (hold: layer), LOOP runs the looper, TAP tempo (hold: freeze), MUTE everything
Knobs 1 to 6Pad volume (press: play the pad), pad tone, reverb, click level (press: click on or off), tracks level (press: mute the tracks), master
Knobs 7 to 10Octave, key, sound and the pads' fade time
Knobs 11 to 14The pads' delay, width, low EQ and high EQ
Sliders A to DPAD, TRACKS, KEYS and GUITAR

The labels on the back tell you what's plugged in: which USB device is in each pair, whether there's a cable in INST A and B, which parts go out of each pair of outputs, the pedal's network address (NET) and how much battery is left (POWER).

Setlists and songs

Open Setlist (under ⋯) and you'll see three buttons. Load setlist shows the setlists your church has made before. Tap one to open it. Create new asks for a name and a day, and lets you add the people playing, with your Central Services teams to search. Add songs brings in songs from Central Tracks, track packs on a USB stick, your own files, or a Planning Center plan. Songs you already own go straight in, and with Central Tracks Unlimited any song does.

When a song comes in, the pedal finds its tempo, beat grid, key and big hits, then lays out the sections. You can fix any of that in the Song editor. If all you've got is a stereo mix, the Stem splitter pulls it apart into stems.

ProPresenter can follow the song. In Settings → Songs & CCLI, turn on Control ProPresenter, type the address of the ProPresenter computer and its port (on that computer: ProPresenter → Settings → Network → Enable network), and press Save & test. Then in the Song editor, each section can show a slide of the presentation that's up (counted from 1) or just go to the next slide. When a song loads, the pedal brings up the presentation with the same name, or the one you type for that song. It needs ProPresenter 7.9 or newer.

Connect Planning Center under Settings → Planning Center and a plan opens as a setlist, with each song's key, tempo, arrangement, chart and tracks. SongSelect lyrics and charts come in the same way. Put your CCLI license number under Settings → CCLI and the copyright line goes on the lyric slides in Central Control.

If a computer runs Central Pedal Control, it keeps the songs and sends the pedal one set at a time. With no computer, the pedal keeps one setlist at a time. When you open another one, it clears out the old songs, except any that only exist on the pedal.

When the song or the section changes, the lyrics and charts change with it on every screen.

On stage: LIVE

LIVE is the screen you'll have up during the service.

The setlist runs down the left. A ● means that song has its tracks. Tap a song to load it, or tap it twice if something's already playing. Next to it you'll see the song's number, key, tempo, the time left, what's coming next, and the Click and Cues switches.

The song's sections run across the middle in order, each one sized by how long it is. The section that's playing fills up as it goes, and tapping another one jumps there on the next bar. Under them is the transport: ◀ Prev section, ▶ Play / Pause, Next section ▶, ⟲ Vamp (repeats this section until you press it again), ■ Safe Stop and Next song. Safe Stop fades the tracks and pads out over 2.5 seconds. Press it again to stop straight away.

The pads, the instrument with its tuner, and the meters are at the bottom. Tracks play without pads and pads play without tracks, so use whichever you need.

Scenes (under ⋯) saves what's playing right now, the pads, the mix and your instrument, and brings it back later from there or a footswitch. It changes over smoothly, not with a jump.

Before the service

Press Ready check about ten minutes before you start. It looks over the sound, the outputs (and warns you if the click would end up in the room), each song's tracks and sections, the temperature, the battery and power, storage and pairing. Most problems come with a button to fix them.

🔒 Lock for the service locks the other tabs and anything that changes your setup, so nobody knocks something by accident. Playing, sections, pads, the instrument and the tuner all keep working. Unlock it with the pedal's PIN.

After the service

The Service report shows how it went: the songs played, any stopped early, safe stops, audio dropouts, the hottest it got, and everything that happened, minute by minute. It's under ⋯, and it's handy if you ever need to tell us what went wrong. Save to the USB stick puts that day's report on a stick as a text file, in a folder called Central Pedal reports.

Pads and effects

Each song has eight pads. Tap one to crossfade to it and tap it again to fade it out. Each pad has its own volume slider, and moving the slider never starts the pad.

The built-in pads play in any key. If you import your own pad (MP3, WAV, FLAC, OGG, M4A or AIFF), the pedal works out its key and shifts it to the song's key. Below the pads there are two texture channels for things like rain, drones and risers, plus octave layers, auto-swell and freeze. Turn on LAYER if you want up to four pads playing at once, and Morph to blend slowly from one to the next.

Auto key listens to your guitar or keyboard. A few seconds after you start playing it hears the key and moves the pads there. Stop for a moment between songs and it listens again. A song with tracks keeps its own key.

Effects on tracks and pads

You can put a gate, EQ, compressor, reverb and delay on any track, any pad, and on all the tracks or all the pads together. The basic version of each is free. Central Plugins adds more EQ bands, the full compressor controls, bigger reverbs, delays in time with the song and the pro presets. A track's effects are worked out once and saved, so they don't slow the pedal down while it plays.

Your instrument

Plug a guitar or bass into INST A or B. The Instrument tab runs it through a board of real pedals, amps and cabinets, in the order the sound goes through them. Every knob is on the screen, and you can put any of them on the pedal's own knobs. The tuner is one tap away.

A USB keyboard plays pianos and synths with up to eight layers, each with its own split, octave and velocity range.

With Central Instrument, Play any sound turns your guitar into strings, brass, a piano or a synth. It follows what you play, single notes or chords.

Looper

The Looper tab turns the pedal into a loop pedal. It starts with three loops, and + Add loop takes it up to eight. Each one can record, overdub, undo and redo, play backwards or at half speed, and has its own level and pan. Each loop can record the instrument, the audio input or both.

The LOOP footswitch runs it. Tap to record, tap to play, tap again to overdub. Double-tap to stop, and hold it for a second to clear the loop.

If tracks or the click are playing, a loop is a set number of bars and starts on the bar line, so it stays in time. With nothing playing, you tap to start and tap again to close the loop, and the first loop sets the length for the others. The looper has its own row in Settings → Output routing, and it never stops your tracks or pads.

Giving the controls new jobs

Any footswitch, knob or slider can do any job in the app: play a pad, switch tabs, set a level, mute a group, run the looper or switch a pedal on your board. Each one can have another job when you hold it (a knob: when you press it). You set all this on the Configure tab, and the label screens show the new jobs straight away.

Touch select is a handy job for a knob. Tap any setting on any screen, a pedal's knob, a track fader, a pad's sound, and that knob changes it.

Under Settings → Setups you can save the whole lot under a name, like "Sunday" or "Youth night", and bring it back with one tap.

Adding your own controls

Go to the Learn tab, add a new control, pick Button, Knob or Slider and give it a name. For a button, press it once. For a knob, turn it all the way left and then all the way right. For a slider, push it all the way down and then all the way up. It's saved under that name and you can give it a job on the Configure tab. This works with MIDI foot controllers and footswitches, USB pedals and any key on a computer keyboard.

A MIDI keyboard plays the pads in that key, or the keys sounds on the Instrument tab. CC 64 holds freeze or sustain. The pedal can follow MIDI clock for the tempo, and send MIDI out to change patches or run lights on the beat (Settings → Tempo and MIDI out). For a Stream Deck, plug it into a USB port and give each key a job and a look on the Configure tab.

Settings → Keyboard shortcuts lists the keys that work on a computer keyboard. You can change the colours too, under Settings → Look. There are a few ready-made looks and a button to go back to ours.

Outputs and sound

OutputsWhere
1 to 6The six 1/4-inch jacks on the back, in pairs: OUT 1-2, 3-4 and 5-6
7 and upA USB audio interface, as soon as it's plugged in

In Settings → Output routing you decide where the tracks, pads, click, instrument and looper go, on one output or a pair. Several of them can share an output. A usual setup is the click and cues on OUT 1-2 for the in-ears, the tracks on 3-4 and the pads on 5-6 for front of house. On the Mixer, each track can also go out on its own output.

Can't hear something? In Output routing, press an output's number and the pedal plays a tone on it. If you hear the tone but not the music, the routing's the problem. If you don't hear the tone, check the cable and whatever it's plugged into.

A USB audio interface works both ways. Its outputs come after the pedal's own, and its inputs can feed the instrument and the pads' audio input. You choose them on the I/O tab.

Central Control can get every track on its own channel over the network for the in-ear mixes. Turn it on under I/O → Audio to Central Control. Ethernet is best. If you have to use a busy Wi-Fi, pick Saver.

The I/O port

The network jack on the right side of the pedal is the I/O port. It's only for Central I/O, which is coming in July 2027. Run a network cable from it to the jack marked PEDAL on the Central I/O controller, and the pedal's outputs can go out on the output boxes, each stem on its own channel at the desk. Never plug the I/O port into your church's network. Use NET on the back for that.

Another pedal can be a backup. Give it the Backup job from Central Control and it follows the first pedal without making a sound. If the first one stops, it carries on from the same spot.

Battery, power and updates

The pedal runs from a 45 W USB-C power supply and charges the battery inside while it's plugged in. If the cable comes out mid-song, it keeps playing on the battery. The POWER label on the back shows how much is left, and Settings → Battery and power shows the supply and the charge in detail.

To turn it off, use Settings → Turn off → Shut down, or press the power button on the back. Press it again to turn the pedal back on.

The pedal gets every update for free. It checks when it starts and twice a day, downloads a new version in the background, and puts it on the next time it starts. To put it on sooner, go to Settings → About → Install now; the pedal starts again by itself in about a minute. It never updates while something is playing, and your songs and settings stay as they are. Each pedal is registered with me before it ships, and only registered pedals get updates.

Back up to the USB stick (Settings → Backup & restore) saves your songs and their audio, charts, lyrics, pads, sounds, setlists and settings. Restore puts a backup back.

Troubleshooting

  • It says it's playing but there's no sound. In Settings → Output routing, press the output's number. If the tone plays, check where the music is routed. If it doesn't, check the cable and whatever it's plugged into.
  • A knob, footswitch or label screen isn't doing anything. Look under Settings → Central Pedal. It should say "Controls chip: answering". If it doesn't, shut the pedal down, wait ten seconds and turn it back on. If that doesn't fix it, write to me.
  • The label screens are too bright or too dim. Settings → Brightness → Label glasses.
  • The battery isn't charging. Use a USB-C supply that gives 45 W or more. A phone charger can't charge it. Settings → Battery and power tells you what the supply is giving.
  • The Wi-Fi won't connect. Settings → Wi-Fi tells you why, whether that's the wrong password or being out of range. A cable in NET always works.
  • A package key won't go on. It works on three of your devices, each a different app, so it can't go on two pedals. Release it from the other one (or under My products on the website). If it says the plan has ended, renew it on the website. If your church gave you the key and has taken it back, ask your main admin.
  • Central Pedal Control or Central Control can't find the pedal. They need to be on the same network, and the PIN has to match. Check it under Settings → This pedal's PIN.
  • The pedal says "This pedal can't start". The small chip that identifies your pedal isn't answering or doesn't match. Shut the pedal down, wait ten seconds and turn it back on. If it still says that, write to me with the number it shows.
  • ProPresenter doesn't follow. Settings → Songs & CCLI says why. The pedal and the ProPresenter computer need to be on the same network, and the port has to match the one in ProPresenter's network settings.
  • Still stuck? Write to us through Contact support and tell us what happened. The Service report (under ⋯) helps: save it to a USB stick and attach it.
