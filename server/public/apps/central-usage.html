What it's for

Central Usage is the musicians' and singers' app. It shows your chord chart (or your instrument's part) and the lyrics, plays your instrument through the rig, and lets you set your own in-ear mix.

Everything in it comes from the main admin's computer, the hub, over the church Wi-Fi. It follows the song and the section the pedal is playing, so you don't turn pages or change songs: the music does.

Central Usage only has the user side. A device running it can't become an admin, and the main admin's Devices tab shows which devices run it.

Your key and your devices

Central Usage needs a U key (a user key). Each one works on two devices, for example a church iPad and your phone at home, both signed in with the same account and sharing the same settings. It comes with ten full releases of updates, and $20 renews it for ten more. It never stops working if you don't.

Your main admin adds you on the website's Teams page. You get an email with a link to make your account, the download link and your key.

More sounds: the starter pianos, synths and pedals are free. The sound plans add more: Central Keys (pianos and synths), Central Plugins (EQ, compressors, reverb, delay and more), Central Instrument (bass and guitar sounds, and what your instrument can become), Central Pedals and Central Pads (which comes with every plan). Each one is a package key by email, bought yourself or given to you by your church on the Teams page. Open Settings → Packages → Add a package and paste it; a package key works on three of your devices at a time, each a different app (this Central Usage, your Central Control and your Central Pedal, say).

First-time setup

  1. Install the app (or open it in Safari on an iPhone or iPad, below).
  2. Sign in with your own account (each user has their own) and type your U key, the 16 letters and digits from your email.
  3. Join the church Wi-Fi, the same network as the main admin's computer.
  4. Find the main admin. The app looks for the hub by itself. If your network hides it, type the hub's address: it's in the main admin's Central Control, Settings → This computer.
  5. Pick User 1 or User 2 and type the pairing PIN your main admin gives you.

iPhone and iPad, in Safari

  1. Join the same Wi-Fi as the main admin's computer.
  2. Open http://<main admin's address>:8095/cu/ in Safari. With a Central Pedal, http://padpedal.local:8080/cu/ works too.
  3. Choose User 1 or User 2 and type the pairing PIN.
  4. Share → Add to Home Screen. It now opens full screen, like an app, and it always runs the hub's current version.

User 1 and User 2

Switch any time with the buttons in the top bar.

  • User 1 (most players): Chord chart, Instrument, Studio (on a computer), Lyrics, In-ears (your mix), I/O and Controls.
  • User 2 (singers, or a second screen): Lyrics, Chord chart for any instrument, and In-ears (your mix).

Chord chart and parts

Pick how you read it: a chord chart, a number chart (Nashville numbers), sheet music or just the words. Then pick your instrument, so it's written in your key (concert pitch, B♭, E♭ or F), your clef (treble, bass, alto or tenor) and a capo if you play guitar. Your device remembers your choice.

If the leader changes the key or the tempo, or an edit moves a section, your chart changes with it.

Part, next to the instrument, shows your instrument's part instead of the chart:

  • Guitar: chart with capo and a diagram for every chord shape, or tab.
  • Bass: notation or tab. Keys: both hands. Vocals: the melody with the words under the notes.
  • Trumpet, clarinet, alto and tenor sax, horn in F, trombone, each in its own key, plus violin, cello and a drum chart.

When the pedal has listened to the song's tracks, your part is what that track really plays. Otherwise it's written from the chords. Parts follow the key the pedal is playing in.

Lyrics

Big, with what comes next. They follow the pedal section by section.

Your in-ear mix

  1. Pick your mix. Free mixes can be taken, and your levels stay with your mix.
  2. Set your levels for the channels the sound desk sent you: more click, less keys, more of yourself.
  3. Choose where you hear it. Local plays it on this device's headphones, with a delay slider. Cloud asks the sound desk to send it to your wired in-ear pack.

You can only change your own mix. Leave this mix frees it for someone else. If your mix "doesn't go anywhere yet", ask the sound desk to send it to your pack or to this device.

Talk to the sound desk reaches only the desk's headphones, never anyone's in-ears. When the main admin talks to the band you see "🎙 name talking" and the music dims.

Instrument

On a computer (User 1), plug your guitar into an audio interface or a keyboard into USB and Central Usage is your rig: the pedalboard, board presets, the keys sounds with up to 8 layers and splits, the tuner, an IR loader and a looper. Your own plugins sit next to the built-in pedals: VST3 and CLAP on Windows, Mac and Linux, Audio Units on the Mac, LV2 on Linux. Add pedal → Your plugins for effects; Piano & keys → Your instruments for Kontakt, Keyscape, Pianoteq and others.

Studio

On a computer, the Studio tab is a place to write and record parts, like a small Logic or Ableton. It's free with Central Usage.

  • Up to four MIDI devices at once: a keyboard, drum pads, a Launchpad or a loop controller. Each one plays the track you pick, and a Launchpad lights up with the clips. With nothing plugged in, your computer keys play notes.
  • Tracks: instrument tracks with up to four sounds layered or split by key or by how hard you play, drum tracks, and audio tracks that record your input in time with the click.
  • Two ways to work: a grid of clips and scenes that start on the next bar, or a timeline where you drag, stretch and loop clips.
  • Editing: a piano roll, a drum step editor, quantize, transpose, and undo and redo.
  • Mixing: volume, pan, mute, solo and meters, with a gate, EQ, compressor, reverb and delay on every track.
  • Export the whole mix, or one WAV per track. Projects save on your computer.

Five sounds come free, and the Studio can use any sound bank on your computer. The plan sounds and the better effects open with the same sound plans as everywhere else; until then a free sound stands in.

What you play live has very little delay, but you hear it without its effects. You hear them when it plays back, or you can set a track to play live through its effects, with a bit more delay.

Controls and colours

On a computer, the Controls tab lets a MIDI footswitch, a controller, a Stream Deck or a computer key do things for you: next section, your in-ear level, the tuner, a pedal on your board. Press Add new control, pick Button, Knob or Slider, name it, then press it once (or turn or slide it from one end to the other). Then give it a job.

Settings → Colours changes the colour of everything. Pick one of the ready-made looks or set your own, and Back to our colours undoes it all.

Troubleshooting

  • It can't find the main admin. Check you're on the church Wi-Fi, not mobile data or a guest network, and that Central Control is open on the hub. Type the hub's address if your network hides it.
  • "This key is now used on another device." A U key works on two devices. Release one in Settings → License, or on the website under Account.
  • Your chart doesn't move. It follows the track player. If nothing is playing, it stays on the song that's loaded.
  • Your in-ears lag. Local listening over Wi-Fi has a little delay: move the delay slider, or ask for a wired pack.
  • Forgot your password? Every sign-in has Forgot password?
