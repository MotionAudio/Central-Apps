What I'm building next: the Control Box

The Control Box is the brain of Central I/O. It sits at your sound desk with the pedal and Central Control, and one box does three jobs: more outputs from the pedal, in-ear mixes for the band, and a mix for every speaker in the room. It comes out on Kickstarter in July 2027. None of it is for sale yet, and details will change before it ships.

The Control BoxThree ways to use it

The Control Box

One box at your sound desk. Its software decides what it does.

Kickstarter, July 2027

The Control Box connects to your church network, your sound board and the Central Pedal, and it tells every input box and output box what to do. Inside is a state-of-the-art chip. The hardware is the same whatever you use it for. You pick its job by picking its software: The Pedal, The In-Ears or Central Wave.

It's a black 1U box with gold lettering, only 140 mm deep, so it fits a shallow rack. One long 6.9-inch colour screen runs across the front. It shows what the box is doing, every channel's level and how fast each network link is running. A push-knob pages through it, and a USB-C port on the front lets you plug in a computer to set it up.

Three networks

Locking network jacks (etherCON): NETWORK for the church network and your sound board, PEDAL for the Central Pedal. A third link runs to the input and output boxes: up through the stack on the rack version, or from a BOXES jack on the standalone.

Talks to your sound board

AES67 to and from your sound board over the network. Dante boards work in their AES67 mode.

Up to 16 boxes

Add input boxes and output boxes in any mix, up to 16, and set them all up from Central Control.

Plugins on every mix

It shapes each mix with our own plugins or VST3 plugins you've installed, and you can change any of it by hand.

A label over every jack

A small screen above each jack on the back shows what it's for and what's plugged in, like the pedal.

Set up from Central Control

Plug it in and it shows up in Central Control, where you set up every box, every output and every mix.

Rack or standalone

Rack

19-inch, 1U and 140 mm deep. It sits at the bottom of a stack and powers every box on top through a row of spring pins on its lid, so there are no cables between the boxes. Before it turns the power on, it counts the boxes and checks the stack.

Standalone

The same front, screen and software in a desktop box, 140 mm deep. It has its own locking power adapter and a locking BOXES jack for standalone input and output boxes, so it goes wherever a rack can't.

Pictures are renders of the design. The finished box may look a little different.

Three ways to use it

The Control Box is the same for all three. Only its software changes.

The Pedal

July 2027

Every channel the pedal plays, on its own output.

The pedal has six outputs on the back. With the Control Box, each stem, the click, the cues and the pads get their own output at the desk. Run one network cable from the pedal's I/O port to the Control Box, stack an output box on top, and spread the pedal's channels across it in Central Control.

  • One network cable from the pedal to the Control Box
  • 8 outputs per output box
  • Set up in Central Control, not at the box

You need: The Control Box and an output box.

How the boxes do it

The In-Ears

July 2027

The band's in-ear mixes, each musician running their own.

The Control Box builds up to 32 stereo in-ear mixes from your sound board (AES67, including Dante boards in AES67 mode), the pedal and the input box. Each musician sets their own mix from Central Usage on their phone, and the output box feeds the wired packs or the wireless transmitters.

  • Up to 32 stereo mixes, each with its own EQ, compressor and reverb
  • One output box covers four musicians in stereo
  • The input box takes the stage mics and instruments

You need: The Control Box and an output box, plus an input box for stage mics.

How the boxes do it

Central Wave

Software addition, February 2028

Every speaker in the room its own mix, timing and EQ.

Place your speakers on a map of the room and plug one to eight measurement mics into your board. The Control Box measures the room and sets every speaker so the sound is even from the front row to the back wall. During the service it keeps listening and makes small changes, and anything it sets, you can change.

  • One output per speaker or amp channel
  • Measures the room with your own mics
  • Follows the service, or stays where you left it

You need: The same Control Box and output boxes. It's new software, not new hardware.

How the boxes do it

The boxes it runs

Add input and output boxes to the Control Box, up to 16.

The Input Box

Kickstarter, July 2027

8 inputs, for mics, instruments and boards without AES67.

Plug an XLR or a 1/4-inch cable into each of its 8 combo jacks. You set the gain, the pad and phantom power from Central Control.

How it's set up

The Output Box

Kickstarter, July 2027

8 outputs, for stems, in-ears or speakers.

Each output box adds 8 balanced XLR outputs. An output can carry one of the pedal's stems, an in-ear mix (stereo mixes use two) or the feed to one speaker.

How it's set up

Both come as a rack version that stacks on the Control Box, or a standalone one with its own power (the output box runs from a USB-C charger). They come out with the Control Box on Kickstarter in July 2027.
