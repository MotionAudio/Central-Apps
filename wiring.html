Central Control Wiring

Everything you connect to the Pi: 12 buttons, 6 knobs, 2 sliders, an optional expression pedal, the PCM5102A I2S DAC board for the click / cue (in-ear) output, and the optional 3.5″ SPI touch screen. These pins are exactly what the driver expects out of the box; the Learn tab can move any of them without re-flashing.

3.3 V onlyButtons, knobs, sliders and the ADS1115 boards run from 3.3 V (pin 1 or 17). The only thing that takes 5 V is the DAC board's VIN.
Power off to wireUnplug the Pi before connecting or moving any wire. Plug the 3.5″ screen on with the Pi off.

System overview

Sound: four mono outputs by default - 1 tracks, 2 pads, 3 click + MD voice, 4 guitar / keys - spread over the Pi's 3.5 mm jack (channels 1-2), the PCM5102A board (3-4) and any USB audio interface (its outputs come next). Settings → Output routing moves any of them, or switches to stereo pairs. Phones, tablets and the Central Usage app on the Wi-Fi open the chord charts; a projector on HDMI 2 shows lyrics.

GPIO header map

Hold the Pi with the USB ports pointing toward you; the header is on the right edge and pin 1 is at the top, as drawn. Tap a colour to pick out one group.

The 3.5″ screen plugs straight onto the header and covers all 40 pins: everything else connects through a 40-pin ribbon + breakout board, or to the screen's pass-through header if it has one. The screen only uses the pink pins; the rest pass through. With no screen, those pink pins are spare (the encoder uses three of them).

12 buttons

Wire all 12 the same way; one shared ground wire can run along the whole row. What each button does is set on the Configure tab, separately for the Pad layout and the Tracks layout (the defaults are below). Any button can also have a "hold 0.8 s" job. Lit buttons: their LED can go on any spare pin through a 330 Ω resistor and be listed in hardware.json under leds ({"id":"led1","gpio":20,"follows":"b1"}).

6 knobs + 2 sliders (ADS1115 #1 and #2)

ADS1115 #2 is wired exactly the same, with its ADDR pin on 3.3 V instead of GND (= address 0x49), and carries knobs 5–6 on A0–A1 and the two sliders on A2–A3. A slide pot is just a long pot: its two end lugs go to GND and 3.3 V, the wiper to the ADS input. Both boards share the same four wires (3.3 V, GND, SCL, SDA). If a control works backwards, swap its two outer lugs or tick invert on the Configure tab. Jobs are defaults; change any of them on the Configure tab.

Expression pedal (ADS1115 #3, optional)

A third ADS1115 (ADDR wired to SDA = 0x4A) carries the expression pedal; the 1 kΩ resistor protects the Pi from the brief short when a plug goes in. Use a TRS expression pedal with the tip as the wiper (Roland EV-5, Boss FV-500L, M-Audio EX-P on its "M-Audio" setting). The driver calibrates itself: rock the pedal heel-to-toe once after power-on. Without this board, slider 1 is the swell control.

I2S DAC board (PCM5102A) – click and cue output

SCK must be tied to GND (the chip then makes its own clock). Set the four solder jumpers on the back of the board: 1 = L, 2 = L, 3 = H, 4 = L (filter normal, de-emphasis off, un-muted, I2S format); most boards ship that way, but check. The Pi sees it as the "hifiberry-dac" sound card. Its line out goes to the monitor mixer / in-ear system, so the click and the spoken cues never reach the house. The app's Settings → Output device lists the board's two channels (outputs 3-4: click + MD voice, instrument) once it is found.

3.5″ SPI touch screen (optional)

A Waveshare / Kuman / "LCD35" style 480×320 screen plugs straight onto the 40-pin header (pin 1 to pin 1). Turn it on in Settings → 3.5-inch touch screen (choose the type: waveshare35a is the common one) and the Central Control restarts with it. It shows the pads and the tracks transport as a touch remote; HDMI 1 keeps showing the full app (or a big copy of the small screen, if you prefer, in Settings → Screens). It uses GPIO 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 17, 24, 25, so those pins are reserved while it is on; the encoder is only available with the screen off.

Rotary encoder (optional, screen off)

The encoder shares pins with the 3.5″ screen, so use one or the other, or move the encoder to three spare pins with the Learn tab. A bare encoder works too: outer pins to GPIO 10 pin 19 and GPIO 9 pin 21, middle pin to GND, push-switch between GPIO 11 pin 23 and GND.

Parts list

Core

  • Raspberry Pi 4 (2 GB or more) + official 5 V 3 A USB-C supply
  • microSD card, 16 GB or larger (Class 10 / A1)
  • HDMI touchscreen for HDMI 1 (any size); a projector or TV on HDMI 2 for lyrics
  • PCM5102A I2S DAC board (the purple "GY-PCM5102") for the click / cue output
  • Optional: 3.5″ SPI touch screen (waveshare35a type) and a 40-pin ribbon + breakout board
  • Optional: USB MIDI interface (lights + general MIDI out), USB foot pedals
  • Instrument tab: any class-compliant USB audio interface with a ¼″ instrument input (guitar) and / or a USB MIDI keyboard (keys)

Control surface

  • 12 × momentary footswitches or buttons (SPST, normally open)
  • 6 × 10 kΩ linear pots (B10K) + knobs, 2 × 10 kΩ linear slide pots
  • 2 × ADS1115 16-bit ADC boards (+ a third for the expression pedal)
  • Optional: ¼″ TRS panel jack, 1 kΩ resistor, 100 nF capacitor (expression pedal)
  • Optional: KY-040 rotary encoder; LEDs + 330 Ω resistors for lit buttons
  • Hook-up wire (22–26 AWG), female Dupont jumpers, an enclosure

Guitar, keys and other USB gear

The Instrument tab needs no wiring at all: a guitar goes into the ¼″ instrument / Hi-Z input of any class-compliant USB audio interface plugged into the Pi (Focusrite Scarlett, Behringer UMC, PreSonus AudioBox, M-Audio, Zoom… anything that needs no driver on a Mac works here), and a keyboard goes into a USB port directly (USB MIDI) or through a USB-MIDI cable. The Pi lists what it finds under Instrument → Input / MIDI keyboard; "Auto" picks the first one. The instrument's sound leaves on output 4 (Settings → Output routing), which can be the interface's own output 1 - so one interface can be both the guitar input and the stage output.

  • Latency: 128 samples at 48 kHz (under 3 ms in the Pi) plus the interface's own; keep the interface on the Pi's blue USB 3 ports and use a powered hub if several USB devices share it.
  • Acoustic guitar with a piezo pickup: use the interface's instrument (Hi-Z) switch; the Instrument tab's Auto button sets the input gain and noise gate from what it hears.
  • Keys sounds: free SoundFonts are installed (pianos, electric pianos, organs, pads, strings, leads, bells). Add your own .sf2 files or a folder of WAV samples named by note (C4.wav, F#3.wav…) from a USB stick (Instrument → Sounds → Add sounds…), or send an .sf2 from a phone or computer with the Upload page.
  • Lights: a USB MIDI interface (or the Central Usage app on a laptop over Wi-Fi) carries the hits, section notes and MIDI clock to Lightkey, QLC+ or a console.

Power-on checks

Do these over SSH (ssh pi@padpedal.local) after the first boot with the hardware connected, or just open the Learn tab: it shows which board inputs answer.

i2cdetect -y 1

You should see 48 and 49 (and 4a with the expression board). Missing one? Check that board's SDA/SCL wires and its ADDR pin.

aplay -l

Lists the sound cards: Headphones (the jack), sndrpihifiberry (the DAC board) and any USB interface. arecord -l shows the interface's inputs; aconnect -l the MIDI keyboard.

journalctl -u pad-pedal-driver -f

Live log. Every button press, knob move, learned pin and companion device prints a line. Ctrl+C stops watching.
